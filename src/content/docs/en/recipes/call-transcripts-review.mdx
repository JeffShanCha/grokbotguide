**Every morning the Bot reads yesterday’s sales call transcripts, scores each against your framework, and lists what went well, missed opportunities and action items.**

## What you get

<RecipeShot slug="call-transcripts-review" />

## When to use it

- Several sales or customer calls a day and no time to review them
- You want to know which objections come up most

## Before you start

- A meeting transcription tool; the official recruiting guide uses Granola.
- Your sales framework or scorecard as a doc.

## Steps

1. Test it on a call you know well and check the scoring.
2. Tune the scorecard until it matches your judgment.
3. Create a routine for weekdays at 08:30.

## Prompt

Replace the parts in angle brackets with your own.

<CopyTemplate id="call-transcripts-review">

```text
Every weekday at 8:30, read all of yesterday’s sales call transcripts in <where transcripts live>:
Score each call against the attached "Sales scorecard" and list:
- What went well (quote the call, with timestamps)
- Missed opportunities: needs, objections, budget or timeline signals we did not follow up on
- Action items: who, what, by when
Then summarize the most common objection this week and what to practice next week.
Report only; do not send anything to customers or edit the CRM.
```

</CopyTemplate>

## Approvals and permissions

- Report only: nothing sent externally, no CRM edits.

## Notes and pitfalls

- This is one of Prompt Warrior’s morning routines.
- It works for recruiting too: in the official recruiting guide, the Pulse Bot drafts interview feedback against the role scorecard and asks for the recruiter’s rating before submitting to the ATS (Ashby).
- Transcripts contain customer information and every Bot on your account can see shared files; keep them organized and separate.
