**Every morning the Bot reads the last 24 hours in the channels you pick, writes up progress, blockers and decisions you owe, and shows you a draft before posting to the standup channel.**

## What you get

<RecipeShot slug="slack-daily-standup" />

## When to use it

- A small team that would rather skip a daily standup meeting
- Discussion spread over several channels
- You want to know what the team is doing in two minutes a day

## Before you start

- Add the Slack plugin. The Cursor Help Center says it posts as you and only in channels you can post in; another workspace needs its own connection.

## Steps

1. Tell the Bot which channels to read and where to post.
2. Run it once by hand and check for missed threads and correct links.
3. Save it as a skill and create a routine for weekdays at 09:30.

## Prompt

Replace the parts in angle brackets with your own.

<CopyTemplate id="slack-daily-standup">

```text
Every weekday at 9:30, read the last 24 hours in <#product> and <#dev> and write a standup summary:
- What was finished yesterday (with message links)
- What is in progress and when it should land
- Blockers and who can help
- Decisions I need to make
Send it to me as a draft first; post it to <#standup> only after I confirm. Do not DM anyone or post in other channels.
```

</CopyTemplate>

## Approvals and permissions

- The plugin posts as you, so always confirm before it posts. Slack drafts appear as a card with Send message or Discard.
- Do not let it reply in other people’s threads.

## Notes and pitfalls

- It can only read channels you can see.
- The official docs say Slack drafts do not appear in group chats; review them in a one-to-one conversation.
- For one Bot the whole team shares, see "Grok Bot × Slack for teams".
