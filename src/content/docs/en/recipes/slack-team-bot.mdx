**Publish a configured Bot as a Team Bot and it gets its own Slack app. Teammates DM it or @mention it in a channel, and each person’s conversation stays private.**

## What you get

<RecipeShot slug="slack-team-bot" />

## When to use it

- People keep asking the same questions: metric definitions, where docs live, how a process works
- You want to share one good Bot instead of everyone configuring their own

## Before you start

- A team plan; Team Bots are available to team members.
- Permission to install apps in your Slack workspace, or an admin who can approve.

## Steps

1. Choose New chat (Cmd/Ctrl+N) → Create new Team Bot, or use Publish to Team from an existing Bot’s Share menu.
2. Configure Plugins, Secrets, Skills and Files under Setup.
3. Publish with Publish to team.
4. In the Bot’s details, choose Bring to your team’s Slack → Connect Slack workspace, pick the workspace and choose Connect.
5. Invite it to channels with /invite @BotName.
6. Each teammate links their Slack account once under Settings → General → Team Bots.

## Prompt

Replace the parts in angle brackets with your own.

<CopyTemplate id="slack-team-bot">

```text
You are <team name>’s "Metrics Assistant".
Teammates will ask business metric questions in Slack, such as "how many new paying users last week" or "which channel converts best".
When answering: give the number, the definition used and a link to the source; ask if the definition is unclear; say so when you do not know.
Read data only; never change dashboards, sheets or settings.
Save facts the whole team should know (such as metric definitions) to team memory, and tell the person who asked that you saved it.
```

## Approvals and permissions

- Sign-in (OAuth) plugins act as the teammate asking; key or token plugins are shared, so use read-only service-account keys.
- Slack channels, group DMs and threads share one computer for the Bot; do not share anything in a channel that not everyone there should see.
- Before using a personal connector in a shared conversation, the Bot shows an Allow card.

## Notes and pitfalls

- Up to 25 secrets per Team Bot; shared files must be txt, md, csv, json or yaml, up to 256,000 characters each.
- Routines are personal and count against the person who set them up; Slack messages from unlinked senders (such as workflow posts) count against the owner.
- If the workspace requires admin approval, setup shows Awaiting admin approval; use Send request in Slack.
- Ordinary channel messages are ignored unless the Bot is mentioned.
